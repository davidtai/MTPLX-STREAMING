## 0. Headline

> **20 tok/s requires the verify to cost no more than a single 1K-context forward — i.e. W80's window
> ring must erase the memory-pressure attention inflation AND W71 must kill the per-layer routing
> barrier — *plus* deeper drafts (depth 5, which is architecturally free via the markov recurrence)
> *plus* a verify hit-rate ≥ ~0.955–0.97 to clear the SSD wall, every lever stacked at its optimistic
> end. The current physical floor at depth 3 with one draft chain is ~13 tok/s (band 11–17): a depth-3
> verify is a 4-row pass through the same 40-layer backbone, which cannot be cheaper than the ~165 ms
> 1K single-row forward, so even a free draft caps the cycle at ~205 ms → ≤17 tok/s. Today's measured
> 16K decode is 2.24 tok/s (AR); the DSpark path is compute-bound (routing barriers + memory-pressure
> attention), with the SSD only ~20–50 % utilized.**

The gap from 2.24 to the ~13 tok/s physical floor is **recoverable compute** (W80+W71+W65+dedup, all
byte-identical). The gap from ~13 to 20 needs **more accepted tokens per cycle** (deeper drafts +
acceptance rule) *and* a residency/dedup gain to keep the SSD off the critical path. 20 is at the
optimistic edge; the central estimate is 13–16.

## 1. Draft-depth architecture — is it capped at 3?

**Answer: the MTP *stack* is capped at 3; the *draft length* is not — it is `dspark_block_size` = 5,
produced by recurrently applying the cheap markov head. "Depth 3" in every census is a runtime
`speculative_depth=3` choice, not an architectural limit.**

Reference `model.py` (authoritative for the architecture; the spec-decode *loop* is explicitly "out of
scope for this repo — nothing calls `forward_spec`", L129-130, so the loop policy is the port's, not
the reference's):
- `Transformer.mtp` is a `ModuleList` of `n_mtp_layers` (= `num_nextn_predict_layers` = **3**)
  `DSparkBlock`s (mtp.0/1/2), each a set of checkpoint weights. `forward_spec` runs all three **once**
  as a shallow stack to produce one rich hidden. **This "3" is the depth of the transformer stack, and
  it is fixed by the checkpoint.**
- Only the **last** stage (`stage_id == n_mtp_layers-1`) carries the `markov_head` (rank-256 embed+head)
  and `confidence_head`. `forward_head` then draws draft tokens in a **`for i in range(self.block_size)`
  loop** (L1146-1153): `output_ids[:,0]=input`, then for each position sample from
  `logits[:,i] + markov_head(output_ids[:,i])`. So the **number of draft tokens = `block_size` = 5**,
  and they are generated by **recurrently applying the markov head**, not by more transformer blocks.
- `DSparkAttention` handles `block_size` positions via `get_dspark_topk_idxs(win, bsz, block_size, …)`.

Port (`deepseek_v41_dspark_decode.py` L17-24, L763-766, L978-979): "DSpark drafts a whole block of
`dspark_block_size` tokens … we propose `K = min(speculative_depth, block_size)` of them per cycle. The
confidence head drives an optional early stop." `k_request = block_size if speculative_depth is None
else speculative_depth`; `k_cap = min(k_request, block_size)`. The census measured `speculative_depth=3`
→ K=3, hence `depth: 3`, `accept_rate_by_depth` length 3, `drafted_by_depth [73,68,62]` (the confidence
early-stop is why 68<73<62: cycles that stopped short). The served bank config confirms
`dspark_block_size=5`.

**Consequences.**
1. **Depths 4 and 5 are available at trained quality with zero extra draft weights** — the extension is
   the markov rollout (`dspark.markov` = 11.7 ms/cycle at depth 3), not a 4th/5th transformer block.
   block_size=5 is the trained configuration (the noise-token positions were trained to 5), so depth
   4/5 are in-distribution; **beyond 5 is untrained** (would need block_size>5, unvalidated).
2. The confidence head can *extend* toward 5 as readily as it early-stops — it is a per-position latency
   knob (`confidence_threshold`), byte-identical to the verify outcome.
3. The ledger's "keep K=3, K>3 dead" (R1, §4) is an **inherited V4 assumption** ("V4 K>3 dead, d5 accept
   0/86"), *not* measured on DSV4.1. DSV4.1's per-depth acceptance is 0.9315 / 0.9118 / 0.9032 —
   gently declining, still >0.90 at depth 2 — so depth 4/5 acceptance is plausibly ~0.87–0.89 (a Gate,
   §5). The ledger's real objection to deeper drafts is the *verify-union widening*, which is a
   streaming-bytes cost (§3.4), not an acceptance cliff.

**The 16K decode is not slow because of physics — it is slow because of memory pressure.** W73 and W76
prove it: the genuinely T-scaling work (all KV `cache_append` = ~58 ms + indexer `select` = ~10 ms) is
**~68 ms/tok, ≈3–4 % of decode**, and window-29b measured that a perfect O(1) append moves decode by a
wash (1.579 vs 1.585 tok/s). W76 bisected "attention proper" to a **T-independent ~7.3 ms/layer
constant that is *uniform across all four CSA modes*** — the signature of a fixed cost inflated by
memory-residency pressure (a per-op O(T) would differ by mode). W73/W80 name the mechanism: the
full-history window store (`40 × 16384 × 512 × 2 ≈ 0.67 GB`) is re-`concatenate`d every token, thrashing
the Metal allocator near the wired ceiling and **evicting streamed expert pages**.

**Lever attribution (task's mapping).**
- **W80 window ring → attention.** Collapses the resident window 0.67 GB → ~5.5 MB (128× — only the last
  128 rows are ever read). Projected to recover most of the +210 ms attention gap, pulling AR-16K
  attention back toward its ~67 ms 1K value. **Default OFF; the donation/recovery is CPU-double-proven,
  GPU delta pending.** This is the single largest AR-16K lever.
- **W71 pinned device route → routing barriers.** The switch cost is dominated by per-layer device→host
  routing-index sync (`route_stage`: `hot.eval_indices`, `hot.begin_split_route` 508 µs, `allhit_fence_eval`
  2.87 ms). W71 revives the W44 barrier-free device gather over a **pinned-only** LUT (W64 guarantees the
  pinned slots are static, fixing W44's garbage). Default OFF, GPU parity pending.
- **W65 draft compile → draft** (see §3).
- **W81 slots/verify → verify** (transient-slot capacity for the verify union, §3.4).

At 16K the AR SSD utilization is tiny (§6: 1.164 GB/tok × 2.24 tok/s = **2.6 GB/s = 19 % of the 13.42
GB/s ceiling**), and expert-kernel DRAM traffic (4.5 GB/tok × 2.24 = 10 GB/s ≈ **1.6 % of 614 GB/s**).
**So AR-16K decode is neither SSD- nor DRAM-bandwidth-bound — it is host-encode / memory-pressure
compute-bound.** The ledger's "4.5 tok/s SSD-bound" (§1.3) is the *1K* regime, where the switch (82 ms)
dominates and attention is small; at 16K attention takes over and the binding resource flips.

## 3. The DSpark cycle model

### 3.1 Tokens per cycle as a function of depth (conditional acceptance)

With conditional accept rates `a = [a₁,a₂,a₃,…]` (aᵢ = P(accept draft i | 1..i-1 accepted)), expected
tokens/cycle `L(d) = 1 + Σ_{k=1..d} Π_{i=1..k} aᵢ` (accepted drafts + 1 bonus/correction). Measured
`a = [0.9315, 0.9118, 0.9032]`:

| depth d | Πₖ products | **L(d) tokens/cycle** | note |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 0.932 | 1.93 | |
| 2 | 0.932, 0.849 | 2.78 | |
| **3** | +0.767 | **3.55** | **matches census 3.5205 ✓** |
| 4 | +0.682 (a₄≈0.889 extrapolated) | 4.23 | trained (block_size 5); a₄ **unmeasured** — Gate |
| 5 | +0.597 (a₅≈0.875 extrapolated) | 4.83 | trained; a₅ **unmeasured** — Gate |

The model reproduces the measured 3.52 at depth 3, so the acceptance chain is trustworthy; depths 4–5
extrapolate the gentle −0.014/depth decline (in-distribution to block_size=5, but the box α at d4/d5 is
a measurement gate, not a fact).

### 3.2 Cycle cost = draft(d) + verify(d+1 rows) + commit

Census per_cycle (fenced, depth 3): **draft 184.4 + verify 1423.0 + accept 0.35 + commit 3.05 ms**.
Fenced numbers are for *decomposition shape only* — they inflate wildly (verify `stage_sum` 3216 vs
`frame_wall` 1423 ms/cycle) and the census decode rate (0.87 tok/s) is a fenced+divergence-instrumented
artifact, **not** the DSpark speed.

**Draft (184 ms fenced, ~160–261 on GPU per W57/W65).** Three *shallow* MTP stages (each: sliding-window
`DSparkAttention` + a **resident** 128-expert **top-3** MoE) over `block_size` rows, + `forward_embed`
(main-hidden proj over target layers 37/38/39) + the markov autoregression (block_size cheap rank-256
steps) + confidence. W65: "~87 ms/stage for a tiny amount of math = pure dispatch count." **W65
DRAFT_COMPILE** collapses the draft cycle 1591→1145 primitives (−28 %, byte-identical, default OFF) → a
compiled/host-encode-freed draft floor of ~40–90 ms. The draft is *not* the binding term.

**Verify (1423 ms fenced).** A single batched forward of **(d+1)=4 rows** through the full 40-layer
backbone (that is what makes it a verify). Fenced decomposition, per cycle:

| verify component | ms/cycle (fenced) | vs AR 1-row 16K | scaling class |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| attention (reuse 372 + full 69 + reindex 56 + swa 20) | **517** | 1.87× | **row-weakly-dependent** (shared 16K KV read) + mem-pressure |
| moe.routed_switch | **734** | 6.16× | **row-dependent** (gather over rows×top-k) + per-split barriers |
| moe gate/shared/combine | 95 | | mixed |
| hc.sinkhorn 71 + hc.combine 40 | 111 | | row-weakly-dependent |
| head (verify) | 9 | | 1 row |

### 3.3 Row-independent vs row-dependent split (the task's decomposition)

- **Row-independent (shared across the d+1 verify rows):** the 16K KV *read* (the dominant attention
  cost — one row or four read the same cache), the Sinkhorn/HC recurrence (fixed 20 iters), the per-layer
  routing dispatch, and the "attention-proper" ~2 ms/layer floor (W76: ~7.3 ms/layer today, of which
  ~2 ms is the dispatch floor and ~5 ms is mem-pressure inflation that W80 removes). This part is ≈ a
  **single 1-row forward** and does **not** grow with d.
- **Row-dependent (grows with rows R=d+1):**
  - *Switch compute:* `mx.gather_qmm` over `R × top_k` = R×6 (row,expert) pairs (vs AR's 6). 4× the rows
    → ~4× the ALU, measured 6.16× (the extra 1.5× is the per-split host-sync barrier firing per row-group
    — W57: "eval_indices per split not once per 4-row wave" — which **W71 removes**).
  - *Expert streaming (SSD bytes):* the union of R independent top-6 draws. Independence estimate
    (top-6 of 384): U(1)=6, U(2)=11.9, U(3)=17.7, **U(4)=23.6**, U(5)=29.3, U(6)=34.9 distinct
    experts/layer. Verify streams the *cold* fraction of that union (§3.4).

  There is **no per-cycle routing-overlap receipt** (the Gate-0 census is still open), so the union is
  the independence estimate above; the coordinator's measured misses (§3.4) are the ground truth on the
  *cold* stream and imply real overlap is modest (consecutive draft tokens do **not** collapse the union
  to ~6).

### 3.4 Verify streaming — measured (coordinator counters, window-32 served)

| metric (per token) | served AR | served DSpark d3 (per **accepted** token) |
|---|---:|---:|
| expert requests | 240 (6×40) | 1,200 |
| **hit rate** | **0.741** | **0.922** |
| misses (cold records streamed) | 61.9 | **94** |
| **bytes off SSD** | **1.164 GB** | **1.69 GB** |
| layers with ≥1 miss | 30.1 / 40 | — |
| record size | 18.8 MB | 18.0 MB |

**MTP does *not* collapse the bytes below AR — it streams 1.45× the bytes per accepted token** (1.69 vs
1.164 GB) and 1.52× the misses (94 vs 62). The verify hit rate is *higher* (0.922 vs 0.741) — the four
rows and the tight draft→verify locality warm the cache — which partly offsets the row-widening, but the
union still widens the cold read. This is the GLM-shaped risk the ledger flagged (R1/R2, §1.4): on a
streaming bank, MTP pays via **acceptance × the higher hit rate**, not via fewer bytes. **Within-cycle
dedup (R2) is the missing lever** — unique requests are 153,086 / 216,000 = **0.71** of total, so a
union-once read at the gather site removes ~29 % of requests before they hit the cache/SSD.

## 4. Budget table — 20 tok/s at depth 3 (≤4 tokens/cycle, cycle ≤ 176 ms)

20 tok/s = 50 ms/token; at L(3)=3.55 tokens/cycle the cycle budget is **3.55 × 50 = 177 ms** (task: 176).
Allocation, against the physics anchors (1K AR forward **165 ms** unfenced; switch alone **82 ms** at 6
experts / 1 row):

| component | today (unfenced est.) | 20-tps budget | reachable floor (lever) | physically reachable? |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| draft (3 stages + markov) | 160–184 | ≤ 30 | 40–90 (W65 compile) | **no** — floor ≥ 40 |
| verify attention (4 rows, 16K) | ~455 | ≤ 55 | 60–120 (W80 → ~1K value) | **no** — floor ≥ 60 |
| verify switch (4 rows) | ~640 | ≤ 60 | 82–150 (W71 barriers + R2 dedup) | **no** — floor ≥ 82 (1-row switch) |
| verify sinkhorn+hc+gate+head | ~120 | ≤ 28 | 40–70 (R6 kernel) | **no** — floor ≥ 40 |
| commit | 3 | 3 | 3 | yes |
| **cycle sum** | ~1,250 unfenced | **176** | **optimistic ~225 / central ~433** | **exceeds 176** |

**The budget cannot be met at depth 3 with one chain, for a structural reason:** the verify is a
4-row pass through the same 40-layer backbone, and a batched-4 forward **cannot be cheaper than the
165 ms single-row 1K forward** (it does strictly more work — 4× the MoE gather rows, 4× the SDPA, same
KV read). Even in the fantasy where W80+W71 make the four rows cost exactly one row (verify ≈ 165 ms)
and the draft compiles to ~40 ms, the cycle is **≥ 205 ms → 3.55 / 0.205 = 17.3 tok/s**. The switch line
alone (≥82 ms, its 1-row floor) plus the attention line (≥60 ms, W80-recovered) already spend 142 ms of
the 176 ms budget before the draft, gate, Sinkhorn, and the *extra 3 verify rows* are paid.

**Physical floor at depth 3, one chain: ~13 tok/s central (band 11–17).** 20 tok/s is above even the
optimistic depth-3 ceiling.

## 5. If 20 is not reachable at depth 3 — what is, and the ranked levers

The only way past the depth-3 ceiling is **more accepted tokens per cycle without proportionally more
cycle cost.** Note the budget *relaxes* with depth: cycle budget for 20 tps = L(d)×50 ms = 177 (d3),
**211 (d4), 242 (d5)** — deeper drafts buy budget faster than they add verify rows *iff* the verify is
row-weakly-dependent (which W71+W80+R2 make true). Worked floors:

| scenario (all with W80+W71+W65 landed) | verify base+per-row | d3 → tok/s | d5 → tok/s |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| central (barriers mostly gone) | 140 + 15R | 240 ms → 14.8 | 280 ms → 17.3 |
| aggressive (barrier-free, R2 dedup) | 120 + 8R | 192 ms → 18.5 | 223 ms → **21.7** |

So **20 tok/s is reachable only in the aggressive corner: barrier-free verify (W71) + attention recovered
to ~1K (W80) + R2 dedup keeping per-row ≤ ~8 ms + depth 5 + d4/d5 acceptance holding + hit ≥ 0.955.**

**Levers ranked by expected tok/s gain per unit of work:**

1. **Verify-cost reduction stack — W80 ring + W71 barriers + R2 dedup + W81 transient slots.**
   *Highest.* Moves the physical floor itself from ~2–4 (today) to ~11–17 tok/s (a 3–5× that everything
   else multiplies on top of). Byte-identical / quality-neutral (cache policy + kernel scheduling).
   W80 owns 74 % of the 16K gap (attention mem-pressure); W71 owns the switch barriers; R2 owns the
   verify-union bytes (0.71 unique → ~29 % request cut); W81/larger transient slots keep the up-to-24
   verify-union experts resident (the served profile already uses 48 slots vs the bench's 6 — the slot
   count is a measured lever). **This is a precondition, not an option** — depth and acceptance rules do
   nothing while the verify costs 1.4 s.

2. **Deeper drafts, depth 3 → 5 (markov recurrence — architecturally free).** *Medium-high.* +34 % tokens
   /cycle (L 3.55 → 4.83) for +50 % verify rows; net positive because the budget grows faster than a
   row-weakly-dependent verify. Near-zero draft-side cost (markov rollout, block_size=5 is trained).
   Risks: (a) verify-union widening — the streaming-bytes cost the ledger warns of; **mitigated by R2
   dedup + the measured verify overlap** (hit 0.922 already); (b) d4/d5 acceptance is unmeasured on the
   box — **Gate it** (a CPU accept-by-depth census + a HumanEval cell). This is the highest-reward move
   *after* lever 1, and it directly refutes the inherited "K>3 dead."

3. **Quality-neutral acceptance rule (R8 TokenV3 / typical).** *Medium.* Raises α at zero SSD cost (same
   verify union), +13–17 % (L 3.55 → ~4.0 at d3). Lossy by construction → **HumanEval(164)-gated**,
   opt-in only (David's ruling; default stays exact/greedy). Stacks multiplicatively with 1 and 2.

4. **Tree / multi-candidate verify rows.** *Lowest — avoid on this bank.* Each extra candidate row is a
   fresh top-6 draw → widens the expert union worst of all (cost/row = full row-dependent switch +
   attention SDPA + up to +6 cold records/layer). The ledger kills it explicitly ("wider verify = bigger
   expert union = more SSD bytes"; GLM d3 already widened 135→180 misses/tok). Only viable if experts
   were resident (they are not) or if R2 dedup collapses the union to near-AR — unproven. Do not pursue
   before lever 1 + the Gate-0 overlap census.

Ranking rationale: 1 is a 3–5× on the floor and quality-neutral; 2 is a ~+15–20 % that is nearly free on
the draft side and only needs a Gate; 3 is a ~+15 % but quality-gated and opt-in; 4 is negative-EV on a
streaming bank. Effort order = 1 → 2 (gate) → 3 (gate) → 4 (skip).

## 6. Coordinator's SSD-binding questions (answered)

Record 18 MB; SSD device ceiling **13.42 GB/s** (12.5 GiB/s); single-stream realized ~5.6 GB/s, but the
verify's up-to-24-wide concurrent union raises queue depth toward ~8–11 GB/s (ledger §1.5; plateau at
QD≥64). DSpark streams **1.69 GB / accepted token** at hit 0.922.

**Q: At what tok/s does the SSD bind DSpark (at this hit rate)?**
`tok/s_SSD = SSD_BW / 1.69 GB`. Device ceiling → **7.94 tok/s**; realized ~10.7 GB/s → ~6.4; naive
single-stream 5.6 GB/s → 3.3. At the (void) 3.94 tok/s the SSD ran 3.94×1.69 = **6.66 GB/s ≈ 50 % of
ceiling**, so **SSD is *not* the binding constraint at ≤ ~6 tok/s — compute (verify barriers +
memory-pressure attention) binds first.** SSD becomes co-binding at **~6–8 tok/s**; it is a *second*
wall that bites only after levers W80+W71+W65 push the compute floor past ~8.

**Q: What hit rate does 20 tok/s need to stay under the SSD ceiling?**
20 tok/s needs bytes/accepted-token ≤ SSD_BW / 20 = **0.671 GB** (device ceiling) / 0.537 GB (realized
10.7). From 1.69 GB that is a **2.5× byte cut**. At 1,200 requests/accepted-token:
- misses ≤ 0.671 GB / 18 MB = **37.3** → **hit ≥ 0.969** (device ceiling); ≤ 29.8 → hit ≥ 0.975 (realized).
- with R2 within-cycle dedup (requests → ~850, the 0.71 unique fraction): hit ≥ 1 − 37.3/850 = **0.956**.

**So the plan needs verify hit-rate ≥ ~0.955 (with dedup) to ~0.97 (without), up from 0.922 — i.e. cut
cold streams from 94 to ≤ 37 misses/accepted token.** That is R3 frequency-residency + W81 slots + R2
dedup territory; it is *additional* to the compute stack, and it is the reason 20 sits at the optimistic
edge rather than the center. For reference AR would hit its SSD wall at 13.42/1.164 = **11.5 tok/s**, so
AR is compute-bound with 6× SSD headroom at 16K today.

## 7. Verdict

- **Depth is not the cap people think.** The stack is 3; the draft length is `block_size=5` via markov
  recurrence, and depths 4–5 are trained-quality and free on the draft side. Depth is lever #2, not a wall.
- **The wall is the verify cost**, and it is ~90 % recoverable compute (mem-pressure attention → W80;
  routing barriers → W71; draft dispatch → W65; verify-union bytes → R2/W81), all byte-identical.
- **20 tok/s requires** the full compute stack (verify ≈ one 1K forward) **plus** depth-5 drafts **plus**
  a hit-rate ≥ ~0.955–0.97 **plus** (optionally, quality-gated) an acceptance rule — every lever at its
  good end. **The current physical floor at depth 3, one chain, is ~13 tok/s (11–17).** Central estimate
  with the realistic stack: **13–16 tok/s.** Honest read: 20 is the optimistic edge, consistent with the
